# IRIN Tone of Voice

## Purpose
- Define how IRIN sounds when it writes, presents, explains, persuades, and documents.
- Protect the brand from sounding generic, inflated, or trend-led.
- Give writers and collaborators practical rules they can apply without waiting for line edits.
- Ensure the verbal identity matches the visual identity's restraint and precision.
- Create a standard for AI-assisted copy generation and editing.
- Support consistent application across `./messaging-house.md`, proposals, and internal materials.

## When to use
- Before drafting any public-facing copy.
- When editing web pages, proposals, decks, case studies, and outreach.
- During reviews of AI-generated writing that feels polished but interchangeable.
- When onboarding a new writer, strategist, or client partner.
- While rewriting technical or strategic ideas for mixed audiences.
- As a final check before publication or delivery.

## Inputs
- Brand strategy from `./brand-core.md`.
- Market stance from `./positioning.md`.
- Message hierarchy from `./messaging-house.md`.
- Channel context and audience seniority.
- Source material such as notes, transcripts, specs, and case study data.
- The non-negotiable visual cue of Manrope, which suggests modern clarity rather than ornamental flourish.

## Outputs
- Voice principles with clear “sound like / don't sound like” boundaries.
- Editorial rules for grammar, rhythm, and style.
- Channel-specific tone calibration.
- Rewrite patterns that turn generic copy into IRIN copy.
- A working word bank for drafting and editing.
- A repeatable quality filter for future content reviews.

## Owner
- Primary owner: Editorial lead, brand strategist, or creative director.
- Contributors: Founder, senior designer, and product communication lead.
- Review cadence: Every 6 months, with live updates when repeated feedback exposes a gap.
- Approval threshold: Changes must improve recognizability without weakening clarity.

## Quality criteria
- The voice feels controlled, intelligent, and human.
- Principles are easy to apply under deadline pressure.
- Rules support clarity without making the prose stiff.
- Channel differences feel calibrated, not inconsistent.
- Rewrite examples are concrete enough to teach taste.
- The word bank prevents cliché without becoming artificial.

## Example (IRIN)
### Voice principle 1: Precise, not performative
We sound like:
- A team that has done the thinking and can state it plainly.
- Writers who choose exact words over dramatic ones.
We don't sound like:
- A consultancy staging importance through jargon.
- A creative studio trying to impress by being cryptic.
Use when:
- Defining offers, describing process, and explaining trade-offs.

### Voice principle 2: Calm confidence
We sound like:
- Measured, assured, and selective.
- Comfortable being direct without sounding abrupt.
We don't sound like:
- Breathless hype.
- Defensive over-explanation.
Use when:
- Writing headlines, proposal summaries, and founder statements.

### Voice principle 3: Intelligent, in human language
We sound like:
- People who can explain complexity without flattening it.
- Experts who respect the reader's time and intelligence.
We don't sound like:
- Academic theory for its own sake.
- Technical writing that forgets there is a person reading it.
Use when:
- Translating product, brand, or systems thinking for mixed audiences.

### Voice principle 4: Restrained, not cold
We sound like:
- Warm through clarity, not chatter.
- Interested, attentive, and composed.
We don't sound like:
- Detached minimalist posturing.
- Overfriendly internet-brand banter.
Use when:
- Writing outreach, case study intros, and workshop framing.

### Voice principle 5: Candid and useful
We sound like:
- Advisors who tell the truth early.
- Partners who name the issue before prescribing the output.
We don't sound like:
- Order takers.
- Agitators performing bluntness as a personality.
Use when:
- Responding to briefs, framing recommendations, and addressing constraints.

### Grammar and style rules
Sentence length:
- Default to short-to-medium sentences.
- Mix lengths intentionally; one longer sentence is useful when carrying a nuanced contrast.
- Break up stacked claims.
Contractions:
- Use them in most contexts.
- Avoid them only when a line benefits from added formality or firmness.
Oxford comma:
- Use it consistently.
Numerals:
- Use numerals for measurements, dates, percentages, and numbered frameworks.
- Spell out one through nine in flowing prose unless precision matters more than texture.
Active voice:
- Prefer active voice.
- Use passive voice only when the actor is unknown or irrelevant.
Punctuation:
- Use em dashes sparingly.
- Avoid exclamation marks in brand copy.
- Use colons to introduce structure cleanly.
Capitalization:
- Use sentence case for headings and subheads unless a proper noun requires otherwise.
Abbreviations:
- Expand on first reference if clarity benefits.
- Do not lean on acronyms to sound insider.
Parallelism:
- Keep list structures grammatically aligned.
Readability:
- If a sentence cannot be spoken naturally, rewrite it.

### Tone sliders by channel
| Channel | Formality | Warmth | Compression | Detail | Persuasion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Website | Medium | Medium | High | Medium | High |
| Social | Low-medium | Medium | Very high | Low | Medium |
| Internal docs | Medium | Low-medium | Medium | High | Low |
| Sales decks | Medium-high | Medium | High | Medium-high | High |

Channel notes:
Website:
- Lead fast.
- Use sharp headlines and concrete subheads.
- Avoid drifting into manifesto mode unless the page earns it.
Social:
- Keep the same standards, just tighter.
- One clear point per post.
- Never chase slang for relevance.
Internal docs:
- Be plain first.
- Clarity outranks flourish.
- State decisions, implications, and next actions cleanly.
Sales decks:
- Sound assured.
- Translate strategy into commercial consequence.
- Keep every slide line pull-quoteable.

### Before / after rewrites
#### Rewrite 1: Agency boilerplate
Before:
- We help brands create seamless digital experiences that drive innovation and growth.
After:
- IRIN helps ambitious teams turn complex products into clear, premium experiences people can trust quickly.
Why it works:
- Replaces clichés with a specific audience, a specific problem, and a sharper outcome.

#### Rewrite 2: Vague product language
Before:
- Our collaborative process ensures alignment across all stakeholders from ideation to execution.
After:
- We bring strategy, design, and build thinking into the same conversation early, so alignment happens through better decisions, not longer decks.
Why it works:
- Names the mechanism of alignment instead of praising collaboration in the abstract.

#### Rewrite 3: Inflated luxury tone
Before:
- We craft best-in-class, world-leading solutions with uncompromising excellence.
After:
- We make deliberate systems and interfaces that hold up under real use.
Why it works:
- Removes empty superlatives and replaces them with testable standards.

#### Rewrite 4: Overtechnical explanation
Before:
- We operationalize design systems through scalable componentization frameworks and multi-surface governance models.
After:
- We build design systems teams can use, extend, and trust across real product surfaces.
Why it works:
- Keeps the substance while making the line readable outside a specialist room.

### Editing checklist
- Can the first sentence be understood on one read?
- Does the copy name a real tension or outcome?
- Are any adjectives doing work that a noun or verb should do?
- Would this line still sound right if the reader were highly sophisticated?
- Has any phrase slipped toward trendy startup language?
- Does the rhythm match the confidence of the visual identity?

## Common failure modes
- Confusing premium with ornate.
- Mistaking minimal wording for meaningful wording.
- Writing as if every page is a manifesto.
- Using technical language to hide fuzzy thinking.
- Letting social content drift into trend mimicry.
- Editing for polish while leaving clichés intact.

## How to avoid generic output
- Prefer exact nouns and verbs over padded adjectives.
- Read copy aloud to catch stiffness, vagueness, and false drama.
- Keep one eye on `./messaging-house.md` so tone never outruns substance.
- Replace startup shorthand with concrete descriptions of judgment, systems, and outcomes.
- Use the rewrite examples as calibration, not as formulas to copy.
- Cross-check final language with `./brand-core.md`, `./positioning.md`, and `../docs/03_quality-bar.md` before approval.
