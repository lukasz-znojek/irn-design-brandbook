# IRIN Imagery and Art Direction

## Purpose
- Define how photography, illustration, crops, and image treatments should support IRIN’s premium design-and-technology positioning.
- Replace generic “tech visuals” with a more editorial, precise, and human direction.
- Give teams a repeatable way to judge whether an image feels aligned before it enters product or brand work.

## When to use
- Use this document when selecting, commissioning, cropping, grading, or placing imagery.
- Use it for website pages, case studies, social assets, proposals, keynote decks, press materials, and recruiting content.
- Use it with `./color-system.md`, especially the recommended default palette direction, and with `./spacing-grid-layout.md` when composing pages.
- Use it before purchasing stock photography or briefing an illustrator or photographer.

## Inputs
- Message intent: proof, product clarity, founder voice, client partnership, hiring, or editorial thought leadership.
- Subject matter: people, product interfaces, workspaces, artifacts, diagrams, or abstract forms.
- Output medium: web, social, print, presentation, or motion still.
- Approved or proposed palette from `./color-system.md` and typography choices from `./typography-system.md`.

## Outputs
- Imagery that feels intentional, calm, and exacting.
- Image treatment notes that can be applied consistently in design or code.
- Clear acceptance criteria for photography, illustration, and stock selection.
- Fewer generic visual clichés in outward-facing IRIN materials.

## Owner
- Primary owner: Brand / Creative Director.
- Supporting owners: Marketing design, product design, and content design.
- External contributors: photographers, illustrators, and editors.
- Final approval for campaign-level deviations: Founder.

## Quality criteria
- Images reinforce precision, craft, and quiet confidence rather than hype.
- Composition, lighting, and grading feel controlled.
- Subjects look real, capable, and contemporary—not staged in a corporate stock style.
- Image treatments connect back to the approved palette direction.
- Crops leave space for typography without making subjects feel incidental.
- Every chosen image can be defended for message fit, not just aesthetic taste.

## Art-direction principle
- IRIN imagery should feel observed, not manufactured.
- Show evidence of thinking, making, and refinement.
- Favor atmosphere with structure: clean surfaces, meaningful detail, and believable people.
- Avoid shiny futurism, over-abstract 3D gradients, or generic “innovation” symbolism.

## Photography style
### Mood
- Quietly confident.
- Crisp but not sterile.
- Human without becoming lifestyle-heavy.
- Editorial rather than sales-led.

### Lighting
- Prefer soft directional light with clear shadow logic.
- Use natural or studio lighting that reveals material texture without high-gloss glare.
- Avoid flat office fluorescents unless documentary realism is the point and the frame is still composed carefully.
- Highlights should feel intentional, not blown out.

### Subject treatment
- Show people working with focus, not posing with exaggerated enthusiasm.
- Capture devices, notebooks, whiteboards, or prototypes only when they reveal process or decision-making.
- If photographing teams, favor gestures of collaboration, review, or calibration over handshake or presentation clichés.
- When showing product screens, use real interfaces with disciplined framing.

## Composition guidance
- Prefer asymmetrical crops with stable alignment anchors.
- Leave clean negative space for headings or captions where needed.
- Use depth sparingly; the image should still read as precise.
- Backgrounds should support the subject, not compete with it.
- Look for material contrast: matte surfaces, glass, paper, hardware, screens, and architectural edges.

## Illustration style guidance
- Use illustration only when it clarifies a concept better than photography or UI capture.
- Preferred direction: restrained geometric forms, diagrammatic logic, subtle depth, and few colors.
- Avoid mascot systems, cartoon gestures, or exaggerated metaphor scenes.
- Motion-capable illustration should still feel static-friendly and structured.
- Lines and shapes should relate naturally to the icon system in `./iconography.md`.

## UI imagery and product captures
- Show product interfaces in realistic states with meaningful content.
- Avoid fake dashboards overloaded with charts just to “look SaaS.”
- Use generous cropping so screens feel premium and not squeezed into mockup devices unnecessarily.
- Device frames are optional; use them only when they clarify platform context.
- Keep reflections, shadows, and perspective distortion controlled.

## Color treatment guidance
- Default assumption until founder approval: use the recommended option from `./color-system.md` as the image-treatment anchor.
- If the recommended starting palette remains active, grade imagery toward deep ink neutrals with a restrained warm accent presence.
- Preserve natural skin tones and material credibility; brand grading should not overpower realism.
- Use accent color moments sparingly: indicator lights, props, interface highlights, or subtle overlays.
- Avoid heavy saturation boosts intended to simulate energy.

## Duotone and tinted treatment
- Duotone is acceptable for section dividers, social art, recruiting posts, or archival materials.
- Use duotone only when it simplifies the image and strengthens the system.
- The dark tone should usually map to a near-black or primary-dark brand value; the light tone should map to a neutral or restrained accent tint.
- Do not apply duotone to imagery where facial nuance or product accuracy matters.
- If duotone feels like an effect rather than a system, remove it.

## Cropping rules
- Crop with a message in mind: proof, craft, atmosphere, or focus.
- Do not crop so tightly that the subject becomes abstract unless abstraction is the explicit concept.
- Protect key human gestures, interface details, and directional lines.
- When placing type over imagery, reserve a stable low-detail zone rather than fading the whole image indiscriminately.
- Use consistent crop logic across a series so a page or deck feels authored.

## Background use and overlays
- Prefer solid background transitions or subtle overlays instead of heavy blurs.
- Overlays should solve legibility, not create mood for its own sake.
- If typography needs more than a disciplined overlay to read, choose a different crop or image.
- Use gradients only when they support transition between image and layout structure.

## Do / don’t image examples described
### Do
- A close, side-lit photograph of a designer and engineer reviewing a dense prototype on a laptop, with visible annotation detail and calm negative space.
- A material still life of notebook, screen reflection, and printed architecture diagram shot with restraint.
- A quiet office detail where the composition suggests rigor through alignment and texture rather than through branded props.
- A real product capture on a clean background with sharp edge quality and readable interface hierarchy.

### Don’t
- A group of smiling people pointing at a monitor in a bright generic office.
- A blue neon “future technology” tunnel or abstract 3D swirl with no connection to the work.
- A stock handshake, headsets-in-meeting, or arms-crossed founder portrait with shallow-brand cliché styling.
- A fake dashboard filled with meaningless line graphs just to imply sophistication.

## Stock photo avoidance guidance
- Start with subject criteria, not search keywords like “innovation” or “digital transformation.”
- Reject images with forced expressions, oversaturated grading, or suspiciously perfect diversity staging.
- Prefer documentary, editorial, architectural, or material-detail sources over corporate stock libraries when possible.
- If using stock, crop aggressively and pair with strong typography so the image becomes specific to IRIN’s message.
- Do not use images where props, wardrobe, or environments obviously date the work.
- Compare shortlisted images side by side; the generic one often becomes obvious only in comparison.

## Illustration and diagram integration
- Diagrams should feel like an extension of product thinking, not decorative filler.
- Use neutral bases with one accent decision from the active palette.
- Shapes should align to the spacing and corner-radius logic used in digital components.
- Captions and callouts should stay in Manrope and use the typography tokens defined in `./typography-system.md`.

## Asset preparation notes
- Export web imagery at sizes that preserve clarity on high-density displays without relying on oversized originals in layout.
- Maintain a consistent aspect-ratio set for recurring modules.
- Record treatment notes with each selected image: crop, grade, overlay, and intended placement.
- Keep source files nondestructive so palette decisions can evolve after founder feedback.

## Example (IRIN)
- Scenario: homepage case-study strip introducing a platform redesign engagement.
- Image choice: documentary-style close crop of a workstation with interface review in progress, hands visible, no direct camera engagement.
- Grade: neutral-cool shadows anchored in a near-ink tone, with one restrained warm highlight echoing the recommended palette direction in `./color-system.md`.
- Crop: subject weighted to the right two-thirds, left third reserved for a 3-line headline.
- Overlay: subtle 10-15% darkening only in the text zone, not across the whole frame.
- Result: the image feels specific to craft and decision-making, not like generic startup aspiration.

## Common failure modes
- Choosing images for trend value instead of message fit.
- Using obvious stock scenes that flatten the brand into consultancy cliché.
- Applying heavy grading that damages realism or skin tone accuracy.
- Letting image crops fight with typography placement.
- Using too many imagery styles on the same page.
- Treating abstract illustration as a substitute for evidence of real work.

## How to avoid generic output
- Ask what the image proves before asking whether it looks attractive.
- Choose frames with evidence of craft: interfaces, notes, material decisions, real gestures.
- Keep treatment disciplined enough that the subject still feels credible.
- Build recurring visual behaviors—crop logic, tone, overlay restraint—so IRIN feels authored, not assembled.
- If an image could belong equally to a stock-heavy SaaS consultancy, it is not specific enough for IRIN.
