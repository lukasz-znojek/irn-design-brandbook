# IRIN Naming Principles

## Purpose
- Define how IRIN approaches naming across products, offers, programs, methods, and internal frameworks.
- Protect the brand from defaulting to empty abstraction, trend-led tech jargon, or overdescribed labels.
- Create a repeatable method for generating, screening, and selecting names.
- Help teams evaluate whether a name feels premium, precise, and structurally sound.
- Connect naming decisions back to `./brand-core.md`, `./positioning.md`, and `./tone-of-voice.md`.
- Support better briefs and sharper collaboration with legal, domain, and product teams.

## When to use
- When naming a new IRIN product line, service, platform, methodology, or signature offering.
- During renaming efforts after a strategic shift.
- When comparing a descriptive name against a more evocative option.
- Before investing in identity, messaging, or launch materials.
- When stress-testing whether a candidate name can travel across channels and time.
- As a review tool for AI-generated or brainstormed naming lists.

## Inputs
- Strategic intent from `./brand-core.md` and `./positioning.md`.
- Offer architecture, audience, and launch context.
- Competitive naming patterns in adjacent categories.
- Desired level of clarity versus intrigue.
- Practical constraints such as legal risk, domain options, and pronunciation.
- Planned use cases: navigation label, product name, method name, campaign name, or internal codename.

## Outputs
- A naming philosophy grounded in IRIN's brand logic.
- Clear pattern types and when to use each.
- A scoring rubric for evaluating candidates.
- A worked example that demonstrates the method in action.
- Practical do/don't rules for naming sessions.
- A reusable template for future naming work.

## Owner
- Primary owner: Brand strategist or naming lead.
- Contributors: Founder, product lead, editorial lead, and legal or operations partner as needed.
- Review cadence: Revisit when the offer architecture changes or repeated naming debates reveal ambiguity.
- Approval threshold: Final names must satisfy both strategic fit and practical viability.

## Quality criteria
- Names feel intentional, pronounceable, and memorable without sounding manufactured.
- The method balances strategic taste with practical screening.
- Pattern guidance is specific enough to prevent random brainstorming.
- The scorecard surfaces trade-offs instead of hiding them.
- The worked example demonstrates real judgment, not arbitrary scoring theater.
- The final standard reflects IRIN's premium restraint rather than novelty chasing.

## Example (IRIN)
### Naming philosophy
IRIN names should feel clear before they feel clever.
They should carry weight without sounding heavy.
A good IRIN name suggests control, usefulness, and distinctness.
It should invite a second look, not require a paragraph of explanation.
Where possible, the name should leave room for the system around it to add meaning.
IRIN does not need names that shout.
It needs names that endure.

### Naming pattern types
#### Descriptive
Definition:
- States what the thing is or does directly.
Best for:
- Navigation labels, internal tools, operational products, and early-stage offers that need immediate comprehension.
Strength:
- Fast understanding.
Risk:
- Low distinctiveness.
IRIN use note:
- Use when adoption speed matters more than intrigue.

#### Evocative
Definition:
- Suggests a feeling, image, or quality rather than function.
Best for:
- Signature product lines, thought-leadership series, and premium offerings with room for world-building.
Strength:
- High memorability and brand texture.
Risk:
- Can become vague or overpoetic.
IRIN use note:
- Keep evocative names disciplined and grounded in a real strategic idea.

#### Compound
Definition:
- Combines two familiar ideas into a new unit.
Best for:
- Offers that need both clarity and character.
Strength:
- Balanced distinctiveness.
Risk:
- Can feel forced if the join is gimmicky.
IRIN use note:
- Favor compounds that sound inevitable, not invented for cleverness.

#### Metaphorical
Definition:
- Uses an analogy to express how the thing behaves.
Best for:
- Frameworks, methods, and internal systems with a clear conceptual role.
Strength:
- Helps teaching and recall.
Risk:
- Breaks down if the metaphor is stretched too far.
IRIN use note:
- Choose metaphors with structural logic, not decorative romance.

#### Modular system naming
Definition:
- A parent name with repeatable child conventions.
Best for:
- Product families, service tiers, and framework suites.
Strength:
- Scales cleanly.
Risk:
- Can become sterile if the parent system is weak.
IRIN use note:
- Useful when IRIN expects an offer family to expand over time.

### Evaluation criteria and scorecard
Scoring method:
- Use a 1-5 scale for each criterion.
- Multiply by weight to produce a weighted total out of 100.
- A strong candidate usually scores 75 or above and has no score below 3 on legal, clarity, or pronounceability.

| Criterion | Weight | What to ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strategic fit | 20 | Does the name express the intended role, tone, and value? |
| Distinctiveness | 15 | Is it meaningfully different from category clichés and direct competitors? |
| Clarity | 15 | Can the intended audience form a reasonable first impression quickly? |
| Pronounceability | 10 | Can people say it easily after seeing it once? |
| Memorability | 10 | Is it likely to stick without explanation? |
| System potential | 10 | Can it extend into sub-names, tiers, or future offers? |
| Trademark screen | 10 | Does an initial screen suggest manageable legal risk? |
| Domain availability | 5 | Are plausible domains or handles realistically available? |
| International resilience | 5 | Is it unlikely to create obvious issues across core markets? |

### Quick screen rules
- Reject candidates that sound like generic SaaS features unless the use case is purely functional.
- Reject candidates that require perfect punctuation or casing to make sense.
- Reject candidates that are difficult to hear correctly in conversation.
- Reject candidates that lean on overused tech morphemes such as -ly, -ify, -verse, or AI-first gimmicks without reason.
- Escalate anything promising but legally crowded before attachment sets in.

### Worked example recommendation
Recommended direction:
- Control Surface
Why:
- It best expresses IRIN's belief that complex systems should become visible and manageable.
- It feels premium through specificity, not ornament.
- It creates a strong parent for modular extensions such as Control Surface Flow, Control Surface Ops, or Control Surface Studio.
Runner-up:
- Sightline
Why not first:
- Excellent feel, but likely to face more crowding and broader interpretation.

### Naming do / don't rules
Do:
- Prefer names with structural meaning.
- Favor calm distinctiveness over novelty stunts.
- Test names aloud in real sentences.
- Check whether the name can live in a navigation bar, a URL, and a sales conversation.
- Consider how the name works beside the IRIN wordmark and Manrope typography.
Don't:
- Choose names that only make sense after explanation.
- Over-optimize for metaphor if clarity collapses.
- Add techy suffixes to create false distinctiveness.
- Pick a name because it sounds expensive while saying nothing.
- Fall in love before legal and domain screening.

## Common failure modes
- Brainstorming without a strategic brief.
- Rewarding surprise over usefulness.
- Choosing a name that sounds premium but cannot scale into a system.
- Letting internal familiarity bias the team toward obscure references.
- Ignoring legal and domain friction until the end.
- Using scoring as false objectivity instead of structured judgment.

## How to avoid generic output
- Start with the role the name must play, not with word association games.
- Use `./brand-core.md` and `./tone-of-voice.md` to filter for temperament, not just semantics.
- Evaluate candidates in spoken, written, and navigational contexts.
- Reject names built from exhausted category patterns unless clarity truly demands it.
- Run the scorecard, then discuss why the numbers feel right or wrong.
- Cross-reference launch messaging in `./messaging-house.md` so the name and narrative strengthen each other.
