# Campaign brief template

## Purpose
- Provide a structured campaign brief for IRIN launches, announcements, and coordinated communication pushes.
- Align objectives, channels, message hierarchy, timing, and resourcing before execution begins.
- Keep campaigns precise and editorial rather than noisy or overextended.
- Support coordinated use of website, deck, and social assets within one planning frame.
- Connect campaign planning back to `../docs/03_quality-bar.md` and the relevant application guides.

## When to use
- Use for launches of services, case studies, essays, or productized offers.
- Use when multiple channels need sequencing over days or weeks.
- Use when KPI targets and budget decisions need explicit alignment.
- Use when assigning work to collaborators across design, content, and engineering.
- Use when a campaign includes approvals or risks that should be visible early.
- Use alongside `../applications/social-templates.md` and `../applications/website-guidelines.md` for execution planning.
- Do not use for one-off single-channel posts with no broader campaign logic.

## Inputs
- Campaign objective and business context.
- KPI targets or success thresholds.
- Audience definition and segmentation.
- Channel mix and publication constraints.
- Key messages and proof points.
- Budget or resourcing boundaries.
- Timeline, launch date, and dependencies.
- Approval path and legal or compliance considerations.
- References to supporting assets or briefs.

## Outputs
- A completed campaign brief that sets scope and message clearly.
- Channel and flighting plan by audience and timing.
- Defined KPI targets and measurement method.
- Risks, dependencies, and approval checkpoints.
- A working artifact ready for kickoff and weekly campaign management.

## Owner
- Primary owner: Marketing lead, founder, or project owner.
- Channel owners: Assigned per website, social, deck, or partner channel.
- Measurement owner: Marketing or operations lead.
- Final approver: Founder or campaign sponsor.

## Quality criteria
### Required sections
- Campaign objective.
- KPI targets.
- Audience and segments.
- Channels and flighting.
- Message hierarchy.
- Budget and resourcing.
- Risks.
- Approval checklist.

### Planning rules
- Define one primary campaign objective and keep secondary goals subordinate.
- Set KPI targets that match the channel and stage of the audience journey.
- Segment the audience only where behavior or message actually changes.
- Treat channels as roles in a system, not a checklist to fill.
- Give flighting dates and cadence enough specificity to support production planning.
- Build message hierarchy from one core thesis down to proof and CTA.
- Identify real constraints in budget, bandwidth, and dependencies.

### KPI guidance
| Objective type | Example KPI | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Awareness | Reach, impressions, profile visits | Use carefully; not a vanity substitute for relevance |
| Consideration | Time on page, saves, replies, qualified clicks | Strong fit for IRIN thought-leadership work |
| Conversion | Inquiry submissions, booked calls, reply quality | Best for launch or offer pages |
| Retention | Return visits, newsletter response, repeat engagement | Useful for ongoing editorial programs |

### Channel and flighting guidance
- Website often carries the primary depth asset.
- LinkedIn is strong for founder-led commentary and launch framing.
- X is useful for shorter observations, threads, and network adjacency.
- Instagram should be used selectively and only when the visual system adds value.
- Email can be included if it serves an existing warm audience.
- Sequence teaser, launch, follow-up proof, and recap rather than posting everything at once.

### Message hierarchy guidance
- Core campaign thesis.
- Supporting proof point 1.
- Supporting proof point 2.
- Channel-specific framing line.
- CTA.
- Objection handling note if the audience needs reassurance.

### Budget and resourcing guidance
- Note whether the campaign is founder-led, marketing-led, or shared.
- Specify design, copy, engineering, analytics, and approval effort.
- Record any paid support even if the campaign is primarily organic.
- If budget is small, reduce channels before reducing craft quality.

### Approval checklist guidance
- Brief approved.
- Message hierarchy approved.
- Assets reviewed for brand and accessibility.
- URLs and tracking verified.
- Channel owners assigned.
- Publish dates confirmed.
- Post-launch measurement owner assigned.

## Example (IRIN)
- Campaign objective: launch a new precision-focused service offer and generate qualified conversations.
- KPI targets: ten qualified inquiry clicks, three booked intro calls, above-benchmark time on page.
- Audience segments: founders, product leads, and operators at growth-stage software teams.
- Channels: launch page, LinkedIn post, X thread, short deck for outreach follow-up.
- Flighting: teaser on Tuesday, launch on Thursday, proof follow-up next week.
- Message hierarchy: IRIN helps ambitious teams make complexity legible; proof comes from method, systems, and delivery craft.
- Budget and resourcing: founder for voice, designer for assets, engineer for landing page, marketer for scheduling and measurement.
- Risks: approval delay, unclear case-study proof, or inconsistent CTA across channels.

## Common failure modes
- Treating a campaign like a pile of disconnected assets.
- Setting KPIs that sound impressive but do not match the real objective.
- Using the same message unchanged across every channel.
- Overcommitting to channels without the team capacity to execute them well.
- Leaving approvals to the last moment.
- Failing to define what counts as a qualified response.
- Letting urgency override brand discipline.

## How to avoid generic output
- Start from a focused business objective.
- Map each channel to a clear job in the sequence.
- Use proof-rich messaging instead of abstract claims.
- Keep targets realistic, measurable, and useful.
- Plan resourcing honestly so execution quality stays high.
- Review the brief against `../docs/03_quality-bar.md` before kickoff.
- If the campaign could belong to any agency with only the logo swapped, the message hierarchy needs sharpening.
