# Brand audit template

## Purpose
- Provide a repeatable template for auditing IRIN brand consistency across verbal, visual, and channel touchpoints.
- Turn brand review into an evidence-based exercise rather than a purely subjective critique.
- Help identify drift early and create a remediation log with owners and timing.
- Support periodic reviews of the studio's website, decks, social assets, and printed materials.
- Keep scoring aligned with `../docs/03_quality-bar.md` and the identity system.

## When to use
- Use during quarterly brand reviews.
- Use before a major launch or site refresh.
- Use when onboarding new collaborators who need to understand the current standard.
- Use after a campaign sprint to identify system gaps and repair work.
- Use when a touchpoint feels off-brand but the issue is not yet diagnosed.
- Use alongside `../applications/social-templates.md`, `../applications/website-guidelines.md`, and `../applications/presentation-template.md` depending on asset type.

## Inputs
- List of touchpoints to review.
- Current identity references for color, typography, and tone.
- Screenshots, links, PDFs, or physical proofs of the audited items.
- Audit owner and approval reviewer.
- Scoring date and context for comparison over time.
- Prior remediation log if one exists.

## Outputs
- A scored brand audit with notes by touchpoint.
- A 1-5 rubric result for verbal, visual, and channel consistency.
- A worked example showing how scoring should be interpreted.
- A remediation log with owners, priority, and status.
- A repeatable record that can be compared quarter to quarter.

## Owner
- Primary owner: Brand lead or founder.
- Supporting owners: Marketing, design, or operations leads depending on channels audited.
- Final approver: Founder or designated brand steward.
- Remediation owner: Assigned per item in the log.

## Quality criteria
### Scoring rubric
| Score | Meaning | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Off-brand | Conflicts with core identity or undermines credibility |
| 2 | Weak | Some aligned elements, but inconsistent or generic overall |
| 3 | Acceptable | Usable and mostly aligned, with clear room to refine |
| 4 | Strong | Consistent, recognizably IRIN, and executionally solid |
| 5 | Exemplary | Distinctive, precise, and system-setting for future work |

### Audit dimensions
- Verbal consistency: tone, specificity, clarity, and avoidance of generic agency language.
- Visual consistency: typography, spacing, wordmark handling, and palette fidelity.
- Channel consistency: fit for medium without losing the brand core.
- Accessibility and quality: readability, contrast, and production detail.
- Evidence quality: whether claims, stats, or examples are sourced and credible.

### Checklist prompts
- Does the touchpoint sound like IRIN rather than a generic consultancy?
- Is Manrope used correctly and consistently?
- Are color values aligned to the approved system?
- Is the fixed wordmark treated correctly?
- Does the composition show restraint and precision?
- Does the format fit the channel it is published in?
- Are accessibility basics met for that medium?
- Would a new collaborator learn the right brand cues from this piece?

### Worked partial example scoring an IRIN touchpoint
- Touchpoint: LinkedIn launch card for a new service page.
- Channel: LinkedIn organic social.
- Verbal score: 4.
- Reason: the caption names a real audience tension and avoids hype, but the CTA could be slightly sharper.
- Visual score: 5.
- Reason: the card uses Manrope well, spacing is calm, and the wordmark is present but restrained.
- Channel score: 4.
- Reason: the square format is right for LinkedIn, though the caption could be shortened for faster scan.
- Accessibility and quality note: contrast passes, but alt text should be more descriptive.
- Priority: medium, because the asset is strong and only needs light refinement.

### Review cadence guidance
- Audit high-visibility surfaces quarterly.
- Audit campaign-specific assets within two weeks of campaign completion.
- Revisit low-scoring touchpoints first during the next production cycle.
- Use before-and-after captures so improvement is visible over time.

### Evidence capture guidance
- Save links, screenshots, or proof files alongside each scored touchpoint.
- Note whether the issue is systemic or isolated to one asset.
- Separate brand-system problems from production mistakes so remediation is accurate.
- Keep comments concise enough that owners can act on them without a second interpretation pass.

## Example (IRIN)
- Scenario: IRIN runs a mini-audit across the home page, one deck, and three social posts.
- Home page scores 4/5 verbally because the positioning is clear and specific.
- Deck scores 3/5 visually because slide density has started to creep upward.
- Social post set scores 4/5 for channel fit because each asset respects platform cropping.
- Remediation actions include tightening deck slide titles and refreshing one homepage proof module.
- Why it fits: the audit stays concrete and yields action rather than vague taste commentary.
- Follow-up note: the next review would verify whether updated slide masters solved the density issue across future decks.
- Reporting style: findings are short, scored, and assigned rather than buried in essay-length commentary.

- Duplicate the audit table and remediation log as needed for the review scope.
- Preserve historical scores so trends can be tracked over time.
- Add one screenshot or reference link per touchpoint whenever possible.
- Use the top issues field to identify the few changes with the biggest brand impact first.

## Common failure modes
- Treating the audit as a taste exercise with no rubric.
- Scoring too generously because the work is familiar.
- Listing issues without owners or due dates.
- Auditing only visuals and ignoring tone or channel fit.
- Failing to use current identity references during review.
- Making the log so heavy that remediation never happens.
- Mixing production errors and strategy issues into one vague note.
- Auditing too many low-impact artifacts while missing the highest-visibility surfaces.

## How to avoid generic output
- Score against actual IRIN standards, not broad design preferences.
- Use evidence in every note.
- Keep comments tied to audience, system use, and execution quality.
- Prioritize actions that measurably improve consistency.
- Revisit the highest-impact surfaces first.
- Check notes against `../docs/03_quality-bar.md` before finalizing the audit.
- If the findings could apply unchanged to any brand, the observations are not specific enough yet.
- Use the rubric language consistently so quarter-to-quarter comparisons remain meaningful.
- Write remediation items so a designer, marketer, or founder can act on them immediately.
