# Creative brief template

## Purpose
- Provide a full creative brief structure for IRIN brand, marketing, and product-adjacent communication work.
- Help teams define the problem clearly before moving into concepting or production.
- Ensure briefs connect strategy, tone, deliverables, and approvals in one place.
- Support better handoff across design, engineering, content, and founder review.
- Keep project framing consistent with `../docs/03_quality-bar.md` and adjacent application guidance.

## When to use
- Use at the start of any meaningful creative project.
- Use when the work spans multiple outputs such as web, deck, and social assets.
- Use when a founder or client request needs sharper framing before design begins.
- Use when assigning work to collaborators who need one authoritative brief.
- Use when a project includes approvals, mandatories, and success criteria that should not live in chat.
- Use alongside `../applications/website-guidelines.md`, `../applications/social-templates.md`, or `../applications/presentation-template.md` depending on output type.
- Do not use for tiny tactical edits that can be resolved inside an existing task ticket.

## Inputs
- Business context and problem statement.
- Objective for the work and why it matters now.
- Audience definition with segment nuance where needed.
- Key facts, constraints, references, and supporting research.
- Mandatories such as legal lines, launch dates, or asset requirements.
- Tone direction and examples of what good looks like.
- Deliverable list with scale, format, and quantity.
- Timeline, milestones, and approval path.
- Success metrics and how they will be measured.

## Outputs
- A completed creative brief with aligned problem, audience, message, and constraints.
- A single-minded proposition that can guide decisions downstream.
- A deliverables list clear enough to estimate and schedule.
- A documented approval path that reduces churn.
- A working artifact ready to attach to project kickoff and production planning.

## Owner
- Primary owner: Strategy lead, marketing lead, or project owner.
- Contributing owners: Design lead, content lead, engineering lead where relevant.
- Final approver: Founder, client lead, or delegated sponsor.
- Archive owner: Operations or project manager.

## Quality criteria
### Brief sections that must be completed
- Background.
- Objective.
- Audience.
- Single-minded proposition.
- Mandatories.
- Tone.
- Deliverables.
- Timeline.
- Success metrics.
- Approvals.

### Writing rules
- Write the objective as a measurable or observable change, not a vague aspiration.
- Describe the audience with behavior or context, not only demographics.
- Keep the single-minded proposition to one sentence.
- Separate facts from assumptions.
- List mandatories explicitly so they are not rediscovered later.
- Define success in advance, even if metrics are directional.
- Use plain language and avoid filler jargon.

### Review questions
- Does the brief explain why this work exists now?
- Is the audience specific enough to guide tone and format choices?
- Would two different designers interpret the proposition the same way?
- Are deliverables scoped tightly enough to estimate accurately?
- Is the approval path realistic for the timeline?
- Are there references to supporting docs or systems when relevant?

### Handoff checks
- Link to `../identity/color-system.md` and `../identity/typography-system.md` if visual outputs are involved.
- Link to `../applications/website-guidelines.md` for web deliverables.
- Link to `../applications/social-templates.md` for social deliverables.
- Link to `../applications/presentation-template.md` for deck deliverables.
- Link to `../templates/design-qa-checklist.md` if visual review is required.

### Brief depth guidance
- Short projects still require all sections, but they can be concise.
- Larger launches should add supporting research, stakeholder inputs, and risk detail beneath the core brief.
- If the brief drives both content and design, define who owns final language decisions.
- If engineering is part of scope, note any implementation dependencies or technical constraints early.
- Record what is already decided so the team does not reopen settled ground.

### Delivery planning prompts
- What must be true for concepting to start on time?
- Which approvals are content approvals versus visual approvals?
- What source material is still missing?
- Which deliverables are core and which are optional if timeline tightens?
- What review format will the team use: live session, async doc comments, or recorded walkthrough?

## Example (IRIN)
- Background: IRIN is publishing a new service page and launch assets for a precision-focused studio offer.
- Objective: increase qualified conversations with growth-stage product teams over the next quarter.
- Audience: founders, product leaders, and operators who need senior craft without consultancy sprawl.
- Single-minded proposition: IRIN makes complex product work legible, actionable, and premium in execution.
- Mandatories: fixed IRIN wordmark, Manrope only, launch by September 30, include founder contact route.
- Tone: precise, quietly confident, editorial, warm-minimal.
- Deliverables: one web page, three social launch assets, one short deck.
- Timeline: brief approval this week, first concepts next week, launch assets by month end.
- Success metrics: inquiry quality, CTA conversion, time on page, and saved posts.
- Approvals: strategy lead, founder, then production owner.
- Supporting proof points: prior client outcomes, service method screenshots, and one founder note on approach.
- Key risk: launch slips if the service-page copy is approved after design has already started.
- Why it fits: each field gives design and production teams a shared starting frame instead of a vague request.

## Template (blank reusable block)
```md
Project name:
Brief owner:
Date opened:
Background:
Objective:
Why now:
Audience:
Audience tension:
Single-minded proposition:
Supporting proof points:
Mandatories:
Tone:
Deliverables:
Deliverable formats:
Dependencies:
Risks:
Timeline:
Milestones:
Success metrics:
Measurement method:
Reviewers:
Final approver:
Notes:
```
- Add sub-bullets under deliverables when one item contains multiple outputs.
- Expand risks when approvals, legal review, or dependency timing could affect scope.
- Add links beneath supporting proof points when research or reference material exists.
- Use notes for context that matters, not for missing decisions that belong in core fields.

## Common failure modes
- Starting design with only a loose verbal request and no clear objective.
- Writing the audience as "everyone who cares about design".
- Confusing deliverables with outcomes.
- Hiding key constraints in message threads instead of the brief.
- Letting the proposition become a paragraph of mixed ideas.
- Leaving approvals vague and discovering a hidden stakeholder late.
- Treating metrics as optional because the work feels qualitative.
- Writing a proposition that sounds polished but does not guide tradeoffs.
- Failing to distinguish core deliverables from nice-to-have extras.

## How to avoid generic output
- Name the real business tension behind the request.
- Use the studio's actual audience and current context.
- Write the proposition so it sounds like IRIN, not any agency.
- Make constraints explicit and useful.
- Tie every deliverable to the job it needs to do.
- Re-read the brief against `../docs/03_quality-bar.md` before kickoff.
- If the brief could survive unchanged after swapping in another brand name, it is still too generic.
- Fill the brief with current, checkable facts rather than references to \"the usual approach\".
- Ask whether the proposition would still make sense if every decorative adjective were removed.
